🟩 The Power Rule: d/dx (x²) = 2x

Why does the exponent 2 come to the front and decrease to 1? Look at an actual physical square with side length x. Its area is A = x². If we grow the side length by a tiny nudge h, what happens to the area?

Interactive Expanding Square Visualizer Area = x² → New Area = (x+h)²
Side Length (x) 3.0
Nudge Border (h) 0.80
Original Square (x²): 9.00
Two Edge Strips (2·x·h): 4.80
Microscopic Corner (h²): 0.64
Total Rate of Change: 6.80
👉 The Proof in 2 Lines:
Change in Area ΔA = 2·x·h + h²
Divide by h: ΔA / h = 2x + h
As h → 0, the tiny red corner vanishes into zero: d/dx (x²) = 2x!
💡 Why x³ becomes 3x² (A 3D Cube!)

If you have a cube with volume V = x³ and you nudge its dimensions by h, the cube expands on its 3 exposed faces. Each face has area x². So the volume grows by 3x² · h! That’s why d/dx (x³) = 3x². For any dimension n: d/dx (xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹.

🟧 The Product Rule: d/dx [u · v] = u'v + uv'

Why isn't the derivative of u × v simply u' × v'? Because u × v is the area of a rectangle with width u and height v! If both width and height are expanding simultaneously, how does the rectangle grow?

Interactive Expanding Rectangle (Product Rule) Area = u · v
Width (u) 3.0
Height (v) 2.0
Width Expansion (Δu) 0.8
Height Expansion (Δv) 0.6
Strip 1 (v · Δu): 1.60
Strip 2 (u · Δv): 1.80
Corner (Δu · Δv): 0.48
ΔArea / Δt: v·u' + u·v'
The Step-by-Step Proof:

Change in Area = v · Δu (width stretch) + u · Δv (height stretch) + Δu · Δv (corner).
Divide by Δt: ΔArea/Δt = v(Δu/Δt) + u(Δv/Δt) + Δu(Δv/Δt).
As Δt → 0, Δu shrinks to 0, killing the corner piece. We are left with: (u · v)' = u'v + uv'!

🔄 The Fundamental Theorem: Why F(b) - F(a)?

Why do we calculate an area by finding the antiderivative F(x) and subtracting F(b) - F(a)? The secret is the telescoping domino effect.

🧱 The Domino Cancellation Proof

Break the distance from a to b into thousands of tiny step increments:

Total Net Change = [F(x₁) - F(a)] + [F(x₂) - F(x₁)] + [F(x₃) - F(x₂)] + ... + [F(b) - F(xₙ₋₁)]

Notice that every single middle term appears once with a plus sign and once with a minus sign! They all cancel out in a domino collapse, leaving only: F(b) - F(a). And since each microscopic step is F'(x)·dx = f(x)·dx (the area of one tiny slice), the sum of all slices equals F(b) - F(a)!

Connecting The Gears

⚙️ The Chain Rule: Connecting the Gears

Neural networks are not single functions; they are chains of functions nested inside each other:

Input (x) → Linear sum (z) → Activation (a) → Prediction (ŷ) → Loss (L)

How do we find the effect of the first input knob w on the final Loss L? We use the Chain Rule.

🚲 The Bicycle Gear Ratio Intuition

Suppose you pedal gear A.

  • Turning Gear A by 1° spins intermediate Gear B by 3° (dB/dA = 3).
  • Turning Gear B by 1° spins the back wheel C by 4° (dC/dB = 4).
  • How much does turning Gear A spin the back wheel C?
dC/dA = (dC/dB) × (dB/dA) = 4 × 3 = 12

▶ 1. Forward Pass (Prediction)

  1. Weighted Sum (z):
    z = w·x + b = (0.5 × 2.0) + 0 = 1.0
  2. Sigmoid Activation (a):
    a = 1 / (1 + e⁻¹˙⁰) = 0.731
  3. Error / Loss (L):
    L = ½ (0.731 - 1.0)² = 0.0362

◀ 2. Backward Pass (Backprop)

  1. Loss Gradient (∂L / ∂a):
    ∂L/∂a = (a - y) = 0.731 - 1.0 = -0.269
  2. Activation Gradient (∂a / ∂z):
    ∂a/∂z = a(1 - a) = 0.731 × 0.269 = 0.1966
  3. Weight Gradient (∂z / ∂w):
    ∂z/∂w = x = 2.0
🎯 The Grand Chain Multiplication:

∂L/∂w = (-0.269) × (0.1966) × (2.0) = -0.1058

💡 Physical Meaning: The gradient is negative (-0.1058). Increasing w will decrease the error!

⚡ Ultimate Mental Model & Cheatsheet

1. Differentiation

What is it? The instantaneous slope / sensitivity.
Visual proof: Outer border strips on expanding shapes.
Role in AI: Tells which direction to adjust each parameter to drop error.

2. Integration

What is it? Total accumulation / area under curve.
Visual proof: Slicing into infinite rectangular strips and adding them up.
Role in AI: Probability density, continuous expectations, loss integrals.

3. Backpropagation

What is it? The Chain Rule applied backwards efficiently.
Visual proof: Reverse gear multiplication from error back to knobs.
Secret Advantage: Re-uses computed partial derivatives so training is O(N) instead of O(N²)!
