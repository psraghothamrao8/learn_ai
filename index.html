Calculus & Neural Net Backpropagation

Many textbooks teach calculus by asking you to memorize cryptic symbols like dy/dx, ∫, ∂, and formulas like d/dx(xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹. Here, we derive every single formula visually using geometric shapes (expanding squares, partitioned rectangles, rotating circles, and gearboxes) so it becomes intuitive and unforgettable.

🟩 The Power Rule: d/dx (x²) = 2x

Why does the exponent 2 come to the front and decrease to 1? Look at an actual physical square with side length x. Its area is A = x². If we grow the side length by a tiny nudge h, what happens to the area?

Interactive Expanding Square Visualizer Area = x² → New Area = (x+h)²
Side Length (x) 3.0
Nudge Border (h) 0.80
Original Square (x²): 9.00
Two Edge Strips (2·x·h): 4.80
Microscopic Corner (h²): 0.64
Total Rate of Change: 6.80
👉 The Proof in 2 Lines:
Change in Area ΔA = 2·x·h + h²
Divide by h: ΔA / h = 2x + h
As h → 0, the tiny red corner vanishes into zero: d/dx (x²) = 2x!
❓ Why 2·x·h and NOT 2(x + h)?

A common point of confusion: Is the added area 2·x·h or 2(x + h)?
• Area is Length × Width: Each border strip has length x and width h. Its area is x × h. Two strips are xh + xh = 2 · x · h.
• Units Check: 2(x + h) is an addition of lengths (meters). 2·x·h is an area (meters²).
• Number Test: Let x = 10 and h = 1. New square is 11² = 121. Old square is 10² = 100. Added area = 21.
   • Formula 2·x·h + h² gives: 2(10)(1) + 1² = 20 + 1 = 21 ✓ (Exact match!)
   • If it were 2(x + h) + h², it would give: 2(10 + 1) + 1² = 22 + 1 = 23 ✗

❓ Why Divide by h (ΔA / h) Instead of Just Using ΔA?

Why not just ΔA?
• The Car Analogy: ΔA is like distance traveled (e.g. 10 miles). It tells you nothing about speed until you divide by time (10 miles / 1 hour = 10 mph).
• The Problem with ΔA alone: If you don't nudge the square (h → 0), the change in area is obviously 0 (ΔA → 0). Without dividing by h, every single derivative in the universe would just be 0!
• The Ratio reveals Sensitivity: Dividing by h (ΔA / h = 2x + h) tells you the rate of return: for every 1 unit of push on x, how many units of area react? When h → 0, that ratio locks onto 2x!

❓ Why didn't h² vanish right away? Why keep it and divide first?

Because before you divide, h is still a real non-zero number!
• The Honest Math: If you nudge a 10m square by h = 0.1m, the corner piece has an actual area: h² = 0.01 m². You cannot throw it away while it still exists!
• The Magic of Dividing by h:
   • The strips term: (2xh) / h → 2x (the h cancels completely!)
   • The corner term: (h²) / h → h (the power drops from 2 down to 1!)
• NOW you take the limit (h → 0): The 2x stays 2x because it has no h left. The remaining corner h safely becomes 0. Both the square and the rectangle work the exact same way!

💡 Why x³ becomes 3x² (A 3D Cube!)

If you have a cube with volume V = x³ and you nudge its dimensions by h, the cube expands on its 3 exposed faces. Each face has area x². So the volume grows by 3x² · h! That’s why d/dx (x³) = 3x². For any dimension n: d/dx (xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹.

🟧 The Product Rule: d/dx [u · v] = u'v + uv'

Why isn't the derivative of u × v simply u' × v'? Because u × v is the area of a rectangle with width u and height v! If both width and height are expanding simultaneously, how does the rectangle grow?

Interactive Expanding Rectangle (Product Rule) Area = u · v
Width (u) 3.0
Height (v) 2.0
Width Expansion (Δu) 0.8
Height Expansion (Δv) 0.6
Strip 1 (v · Δu): 1.60
Strip 2 (u · Δv): 1.80
Corner (Δu · Δv): 0.48
ΔArea / Δt: v·u' + u·v'
🔍 Step-by-Step Proof: How Exactly is v·u' + u·v' Derived?
Let's trace the math with zero skipped steps:
  1. Initial Rectangle Area:
    At time t, width is u and height is v. Area A = u · v.
  2. After a tiny time step Δt:
    Width expands by Δu to (u + Δu).
    Height expands by Δv to (v + Δv).
    New Area: A_new = (u + Δu)(v + Δv) = u·v + u·Δv + v·Δu + Δu·Δv.
  3. Subtract old area to find Change in Area (ΔA):
    ΔA = A_new - A = (u·v + u·Δv + v·Δu + Δu·Δv) - u·v
    ΔA = v · Δu + u · Δv + Δu · Δv
    (Notice the 3 pieces: right strip v·Δu, top strip u·Δv, and corner Δu·Δv!)
  4. Divide by Δt to find Rate of Change:
    ΔA / Δt = v · (Δu / Δt) + u · (Δv / Δt) + Δu · (Δv / Δt)
  5. Take the limit as Δt → 0:
    • Δu / Δt → u' (the derivative of u)
    • Δv / Δt → v' (the derivative of v)
    • The corner piece: Δu · (Δv / Δt) → 0 × v' = 0 (vanishes completely!)
    • Final Result: (u · v)' = v · u' + u · v'!
❓ Common Doubt: Isn't dt = du · dv? What is dt vs du · dv?

No, dt is NOT du · dv! Here is the crucial difference:
• Units Check: dt is Time (seconds). But du is length (meters) and dv is length (meters), so du · dv is an Area (meters²)! A time can never equal an area.
• What is dt? t is the master clock ticking on the wall. dt is a tiny tick of time (e.g. 0.001 seconds). As time ticks by dt, the width grows by du and height grows by dv.
• What is du · dv? du · dv is literally the area of the microscopic corner square in the diagram! It is part of the added area (ΔA), not time.
• Why we divide by dt: We want to know: "How fast is the area growing per second?" That is why we divide by the clock tick: dA / dt.

❓ "Why 'knob x' and why dx? Do we even need x?"

The secret: You DON'T even need x!
The purest, most fundamental formula for a rectangle has no x, no dx, and no division at all:
d(u · v) = u · dv + v · du In plain English: "A tiny change in Area = (width × tiny change in height) + (height × tiny change in width)."

So why do textbooks write dx?
Only when both sides u and v happen to be controlled by an underlying variable x!
• Example (Plant Leaf): Suppose u is leaf width, v is leaf length, and x is the amount of sunlight.
• If you want to know: "How fast does leaf area grow per unit of sunlight?", you take the pure formula and divide every term by the sunlight nudge dx:
d(u · v)/dx = u · (dv/dx) + v · (du/dx) • In pure math functions like f(x) = x² · sin(x), u = x² and v = sin(x) both depend on the same input x.

❓ Why was the Square so easy with no extra variable? (1 Knob vs 2 Knobs)

Because a square only has ONE knob in the entire universe:
• The Square (1 Knob): To define a square, you only need one measurement: x. Width is x, height is x. The side length is the knob! You don't need an outside variable to control them, because $x$ is already right there.
• The Rectangle (2 Knobs): A rectangle has two independent measurements: width u and height v. You can stretch width while keeping height frozen, or stretch height while keeping width frozen. That means you have two separate knobs!
• Why textbooks bring an extra knob: Basic calculus only knows how to turn one knob at a time. To force a 2-knob rectangle into a 1-knob problem, textbooks tie both u and v to an external master knob x (like sunlight or time). In multivariable calculus, we don't bother with $x$ at all and just write: dA = v · du + u · dv!

🔄 Trigonometric Derivatives: d/dx [sin(x)] = cos(x)

Why is the derivative of sine equal to cosine? Think of a point traveling around a unit circle. Its height is sin(θ), and its horizontal position is cos(θ). When the point moves forward around the circle, the rate at which its vertical height rises is exactly equal to its horizontal position: cos(θ)!

🔄 The Fundamental Theorem: Why F(b) - F(a)?

Why do we calculate an area by finding the antiderivative F(x) and subtracting F(b) - F(a)? The secret is the telescoping domino effect.

🧱 The Domino Cancellation Proof

Break the distance from a to b into thousands of tiny step increments:

Total Net Change = [F(x₁) - F(a)] + [F(x₂) - F(x₁)] + [F(x₃) - F(x₂)] + ... + [F(b) - F(xₙ₋₁)]

Notice that every single middle term appears once with a plus sign and once with a minus sign! They all cancel out in a domino collapse, leaving only: F(b) - F(a). And since each microscopic step is F'(x)·dx = f(x)·dx (the area of one tiny slice), the sum of all slices equals F(b) - F(a)!

▶ 1. Forward Pass (Prediction)

  1. Weighted Sum (z):
    z = w·x + b = (0.5 × 2.0) + 0 = 1.0
  2. Sigmoid Activation (a):
    a = 1 / (1 + e⁻¹˙⁰) = 0.731
  3. Error / Loss (L):
    L = ½ (0.731 - 1.0)² = 0.0362

◀ 2. Backward Pass (Backprop)

  1. Loss Gradient (∂L / ∂a):
    ∂L/∂a = (a - y) = 0.731 - 1.0 = -0.269
  2. Activation Gradient (∂a / ∂z):
    ∂a/∂z = a(1 - a) = 0.731 × 0.269 = 0.1966
  3. Weight Gradient (∂z / ∂w):
    ∂z/∂w = x = 2.0
🎯 The Grand Chain Multiplication:

∂L/∂w = (-0.269) × (0.1966) × (2.0) = -0.1058

💡 Physical Meaning: The gradient is negative (-0.1058). Increasing w will decrease the error!

⚡ Ultimate Mental Model & Cheatsheet

1. Differentiation

What is it? The instantaneous slope / sensitivity.
Visual proof: Outer border strips on expanding shapes.
Role in AI: Tells which direction to adjust each parameter to drop error.

2. Integration

What is it? Total accumulation / area under curve.
Visual proof: Slicing into infinite rectangular strips and adding them up.
Role in AI: Probability density, continuous expectations, loss integrals.

3. Backpropagation

What is it? The Chain Rule applied backwards efficiently.
Visual proof: Reverse gear multiplication from error back to knobs.
Secret Advantage: Re-uses computed partial derivatives so training is O(N) instead of O(N²)!
